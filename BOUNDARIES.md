# What this is not

- Not financial, legal, medical or political advice: the video describes and analyses.
- Not a rate of capability loss for Alpha or for agents in general. One skill (the phone skill) had this mechanism;
  the production counts are four reads of one file, and the runtime test is 72 runs on twelve requests, ten of them
  written by the author. They say what happened here, not how often it happens elsewhere.
- Not evidence that the fix works in production. As of 2026-11-24 the phone skill had not been read in production
  since the split; the fix is measured in the test only.
- Not a claim that the three papers (Voyager, MemGPT, Reflexion) studied this failure, or a survey of the field: it is
  one reading of three papers, and no wider search was done.
- Not a judgement that Alpha's 90,000-token setting was a mistake. Why it was chosen is not recorded where the author
  looked, and a larger window costs more on every turn.
- Not a review of OpenClaw. Its rule (serve a skill file whole or not at all, within a budget) is described and
  measured as it was in version 2026.9.8; the loss came from how this rule met one file's size and one setting.
- The general lesson in the close (a store of knowledge needs a check that each piece can still be delivered whole) is
  a guess, marked "guess" on screen; only one runtime was checked.
- The verification readiness number measures how complete and checked the evidence is, not whether the video is right.
- A model's check of a source is a development signal, not authority. `CHECKED.md` says who checked what.
